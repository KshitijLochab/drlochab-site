Liver

Cirrhosis

By Dr. Kshitij Lochab, Consultant Gastroenterologist · Updated 8 October 2026

Years of liver injury replace healthy tissue with scar. Common causes are alcohol, fatty liver and hepatitis B or C.

Common symptoms

  • Swelling of the belly or legs
  • Vomiting blood or black stools
  • Confusion or excessive sleepiness
  • Easy bruising

Causes and risk factors

  • Alcohol over many years
  • Fatty liver that has progressed
  • Hepatitis B or C
  • Autoimmune hepatitis
  • Rarer genetic and bile duct diseases

How it is diagnosed

Blood tests, an ultrasound and a liver stiffness test assess the liver. An endoscopy checks for enlarged veins (varices) in the food pipe, and an ultrasound every 6 months screens for liver cancer.

Treatment

Treating the cause, screening for liver cancer every 6 months, and endoscopy to find and band enlarged veins (varices) before they bleed.

Diet guide: Cirrhosis

Eat more

  • Enough protein: dal, paneer, curd, eggs, fish or chicken
  • Small meals every 3 to 4 hours
  • A bedtime snack, such as milk with a roti or poha
  • Fresh home-cooked food

Cut down

  • Added salt, papad, pickles, namkeen and packaged foods, if you have swelling
  • Alcohol, completely
  • Raw or undercooked meat and seafood

Good to know

  • Do not cut protein unless your doctor tells you to
  • The bedtime snack helps protect muscle
  • Follow your doctor's advice on fluids if you have swelling

When to see a gastroenterologist

Anyone with cirrhosis needs regular specialist follow-up. Vomiting blood or confusion is an emergency.

Common questions

Can cirrhosis be reversed?

Scarring usually does not fully go away, but treating the cause can stop it getting worse and sometimes improves it. In advanced cases, a liver transplant may be discussed.

Should I eat less protein?

No, unless your doctor advises it. Most people with cirrhosis need enough protein and a bedtime snack to protect their muscles.

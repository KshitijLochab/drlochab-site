H. pylori, gastritis & ulcers

By Dr. Kshitij Lochab, Consultant Gastroenterologist · Updated 10 October 2026

Share on WhatsApp

H. pylori is a common stomach infection that can cause inflammation and ulcers. Painkillers such as ibuprofen, diclofenac and aspirin are another frequent cause.

Common symptoms

  • Burning or gnawing pain in the upper belly
  • Pain that changes with meals
  • Nausea or feeling full quickly
  • Black stools, which mean bleeding

Causes and risk factors

  • H. pylori infection, often picked up in childhood
  • Painkillers such as ibuprofen, diclofenac and aspirin
  • Smoking and heavy alcohol use
  • Severe illness or major surgery (stress ulcers)

How it is diagnosed

H. pylori is confirmed with a urea breath test, a stool test, or a biopsy taken during endoscopy. An endoscopy is advised for warning signs or ulcer bleeding.

Treatment

A breath, stool or biopsy test confirms H. pylori. About two weeks of combined medicines usually clears it. Stopping the painkiller lets the stomach heal.

Diet with gastritis, ulcers and H. pylori

Eat more

  • Regular meals at fixed times, without long gaps
  • Normal home food: dal, roti, rice, vegetables, curd and fruits
  • Curd or chaas during H. pylori treatment, which may ease the side effects of the antibiotics

Cut down

  • Painkillers for body or joint pain unless your doctor advises them; they are a major cause of ulcers
  • Smoking and alcohol, which slow ulcer healing
  • Foods that clearly worsen your own pain, such as very spicy or fried meals

Good to know

  • A bland diet does not heal ulcers; medicines and removing the cause do
  • Cold milk may soothe for a moment but does not heal an ulcer
  • Finish the full H. pylori course and take a test afterwards to confirm it has gone

When to see a gastroenterologist

Upper belly pain lasting more than a few weeks. Any black stool or vomiting of blood needs care the same day.

Keep a symptom diary. Log your bowel habits, symptoms and possible food triggers for 2 to 4 weeks, privately on your phone, and bring the summary to your visit.

Common questions

Do I need a test after treatment?

Yes. A breath or stool test at least 4 weeks after finishing the antibiotics confirms the infection has cleared.

Is H. pylori infectious?

It spreads within families, mostly in childhood, through food, water and saliva. Family members without symptoms usually do not need routine testing.

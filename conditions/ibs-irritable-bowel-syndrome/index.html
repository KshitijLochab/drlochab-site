Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)

By Dr. Kshitij Lochab, Consultant Gastroenterologist · Updated 10 October 2026

Share on WhatsApp

A common condition where the gut is overly sensitive. It causes real discomfort but does not harm the intestine or lead to cancer.

Common symptoms

  • Belly pain that eases after passing stool
  • Bloating
  • Constipation, loose motions, or both in turn
  • Worse with stress

Causes and risk factors

  • An over-sensitive gut-brain connection
  • A past stomach infection (post-infectious IBS)
  • Stress, anxiety and poor sleep
  • Certain foods that ferment in the gut
  • Changes in gut bacteria

How it is diagnosed

IBS is diagnosed from the symptoms, with simple blood and stool tests and a celiac test when needed. A colonoscopy is advised for warning signs or new symptoms after 45.

Treatment

Diet changes, adjusting fibre, managing stress, and targeted medicines. Tests are done when needed to rule out other causes.

Diet guide: IBS & bloating

Eat more

  • Regular meal times
  • Soluble fibre: oats, isabgol, cooked vegetables
  • Curd and chaas if they suit you
  • Enough water through the day
  • Slow eating, chewing well

Cut down

  • Large portions of rajma, chana, onion and garlic if they trigger you
  • Fizzy drinks
  • Sugar-free gums and sweets with sorbitol
  • Large amounts of wheat bran (chokar), which can worsen bloating
  • Excess tea and coffee
  • Very spicy or oily meals

Good to know

  • A structured low-FODMAP diet, done with guidance, helps many people
  • Stress and poor sleep worsen symptoms
  • New symptoms after 45, bleeding or weight loss need tests first

When to see a gastroenterologist

Symptoms affecting daily life, or new bowel symptoms after age 45.

Keep a symptom diary. Log your bowel habits, symptoms and possible food triggers for 2 to 4 weeks, privately on your phone, and bring the summary to your visit.

Common questions

Is IBS dangerous?

No. It causes real symptoms but does not damage the bowel or lead to cancer.

Can IBS be cured?

There is no one-time cure, but most people improve a lot with diet changes, routine, stress care and, when needed, medicines.

Pancreatitis

By Dr. Kshitij Lochab, Consultant Gastroenterologist · Updated 10 October 2026

Sudden (acute) attacks are most often caused by gallstones or alcohol. Repeated attacks can lead to chronic pancreatitis.

Common symptoms

  • Severe upper belly pain going through to the back
  • Vomiting
  • In chronic disease: oily stools, weight loss, diabetes

Causes and risk factors

  • Gallstones
  • Alcohol
  • Very high triglycerides
  • Some medicines
  • Smoking, and in chronic pancreatitis, genetic factors and tropical pancreatitis

How it is diagnosed

Blood amylase or lipase and an ultrasound confirm an attack. A CT scan, MRCP or endoscopic ultrasound shows complications and the cause.

Treatment

Acute attacks need hospital care. Fluid collections, duct stones and narrowings can often be treated with EUS or ERCP instead of open surgery.

Diet guide: After pancreatitis

Eat more

  • In the first few weeks: small, frequent, lighter meals
  • Boiled, steamed or lightly cooked food
  • Protein: dal, curd, paneer, eggs, fish, chicken
  • Plenty of fluids

Cut down

  • Alcohol, completely and for good
  • Smoking
  • Fried food and very large meals
  • Sugar, sweets and sweet drinks if triglycerides or blood sugar are high

Good to know

  • After a single attack, most people can return to a normal, balanced diet once recovered
  • In chronic pancreatitis, do not cut fat drastically; enzyme capsules with meals help digest it
  • If gallstones caused the attack, removing the gallbladder prevents another
  • Sudden severe pain with vomiting needs emergency care

When to see a gastroenterologist

Severe belly pain with vomiting needs emergency care.

Common questions

Can pancreatitis come back?

Yes, if the cause continues. Removing the gallbladder after gallstone pancreatitis and stopping alcohol and smoking greatly reduce the risk.

Can pancreatitis cause diabetes?

Yes. Repeated damage in chronic pancreatitis can reduce insulin, so blood sugar is checked regularly.

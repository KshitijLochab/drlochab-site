Variceal banding

By Dr. Kshitij Lochab, Consultant Gastroenterologist · Updated 17 October 2026

In liver disease, veins in the food pipe can swell and bleed. Small rubber bands placed through the scope shrink them and prevent or stop bleeding.

How it works

In cirrhosis and some other liver diseases, pressure builds up in the veins that carry blood to the liver. Blood finds other routes, and the veins in the lower food pipe swell. These are called varices. Large varices can burst and cause heavy, dangerous bleeding.

Variceal banding (endoscopic variceal ligation, EVL) places tiny rubber bands around these veins through the scope. The banded vein shrinks, scars and falls off within days. Banding is done to prevent a first bleed in large varices, to stop active bleeding, and to prevent repeat bleeding.

What it is used for

  • Preventing a first bleed from varices in cirrhosis
  • Stopping active bleeding from varices
  • Preventing repeat bleeding

Before the procedure

  • Nothing to eat for 6 to 8 hours before.
  • Blood tests such as platelet count and clotting are checked.
  • Tell the doctor about all your medicines, including blood thinners and diuretics.
  • Come with an adult, as sedation is used.

On the day

You are given a throat spray and sedation. A small cap with bands is fitted to the scope, each vein is gently sucked into the cap and a band is released around it. Several bands may be placed in one session, which takes about 15 to 20 minutes.

After the procedure

  • Mild chest discomfort and pain on swallowing for 1 to 2 days are common.
  • Take cold liquids for the first few hours, then a soft diet for a day or two, before returning to normal food.
  • An acid-reducing medicine is sometimes given to help the small banding ulcers heal.
  • Banding is repeated every 2 to 4 weeks until the varices are gone, followed by check-up endoscopies.
  • Continue liver medicines as prescribed, and avoid alcohol completely.

Risks

Chest discomfort and mild pain on swallowing are common and settle quickly. Bleeding from the small ulcer left after a band falls off is uncommon but can be serious, so any sign of bleeding needs urgent care. A narrowing of the food pipe is rare.

Get medical help straight away if you have

  • Vomiting blood or black, tarry stools
  • Dizziness, fainting or a racing heartbeat
  • Severe chest pain or being unable to swallow

Other tests and options

Medicines called beta-blockers lower the pressure in the liver veins and are often used with, or instead of, banding. Varices in the stomach are often treated with glue injection. If bleeding keeps coming back, a TIPS procedure done by a radiologist may be considered.

Common questions

How many sessions are needed?

Usually several, 2 to 4 weeks apart, until the veins are gone. Checks continue afterwards.

Will I also need medicines?

Often yes. Medicines that lower pressure in the liver veins (beta-blockers) are commonly used with banding.

Does banding cure cirrhosis?

No. Banding treats the swollen veins and lowers the risk of bleeding. Treating the cause of liver disease and regular follow-up remain essential.

Is banding painful?

It is done under sedation. Afterwards, some chest discomfort or pain on swallowing for a day or two is common.

Who needs banding?

People with cirrhosis who have large varices, those who have bled from varices, and some with high-risk features on endoscopy. Everyone with cirrhosis should have a screening endoscopy to check.

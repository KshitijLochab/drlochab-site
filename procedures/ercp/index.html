Bile & pancreatic ducts

ERCP

By Dr. Kshitij Lochab, Consultant Gastroenterologist · Updated 17 October 2026

Share on WhatsApp

A scope with X-ray guidance reaches the opening of the bile and pancreatic ducts to remove stones and place stents through blockages, without any cuts.

How it works

ERCP (endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography) treats problems of the bile duct and pancreatic duct without open surgery. A side-viewing scope is passed through the mouth to the opening of the bile duct in the duodenum. A thin tube is passed into the duct, dye is injected and X-ray pictures show stones or narrowings.

Through the scope, the opening can be widened with a small cut (sphincterotomy), stones can be pulled out with a balloon or basket, and plastic or metal stents can be placed to drain a blocked duct. This quickly relieves jaundice, itching and infection of the bile duct (cholangitis).

Because ERCP is a treatment procedure, scans such as MRCP or EUS are usually done first to confirm that it is needed. Dr. Kshitij Lochab has an Advanced Fellowship in EUS and ERCP.

What it is used for

  • Stones stuck in the bile duct
  • Blockage of the bile duct by a narrowing or growth (stenting)
  • Bile leaks after surgery
  • Stones and narrowings in the pancreatic duct

Before the procedure

  • Nothing to eat for 6 to 8 hours before.
  • Blood tests, including liver tests and clotting, are checked beforehand.
  • Tell the doctor about blood thinners, diabetes medicines, allergies (including to X-ray dye or iodine), and if you may be pregnant.
  • ERCP is usually done as a day-care or overnight hospital stay. A family member should come with you.
  • The benefits and risks are explained, and you are asked to sign a consent form.

On the day

You lie on your tummy or side and are given deep sedation or anaesthesia. The procedure uses X-rays, so staff wear lead aprons. It usually takes 30 to 90 minutes, depending on what needs to be done, such as removing several stones or placing a stent.

After the procedure

  • You are watched for several hours, and many people stay overnight.
  • Nothing is eaten for a few hours; then clear fluids are started, followed by food if you feel well.
  • Mild throat soreness and bloating are common.
  • If a plastic stent was placed, a repeat ERCP to change or remove it is usually planned within about 3 months. Do not miss this date.
  • If stones came from the gallbladder, removal of the gallbladder is often advised to stop them coming back.

Risks

ERCP is more complex than a routine endoscopy. Pancreatitis is the most common complication, occurring in a few out of every 100 people; it is usually mild but can be serious. Bleeding after sphincterotomy, infection and a tear in the duodenum are uncommon. Steps are taken to lower these risks, and they are explained before the procedure.

Get medical help straight away if you have

  • Severe belly pain, especially going through to the back
  • Fever with shivering, or worsening yellow eyes
  • Vomiting blood or black stools

Other tests and options

MRCP (a special MRI) and EUS can diagnose bile duct stones and narrowings but cannot treat them. If ERCP is not possible, a bile duct can be drained through the skin by a radiologist (PTBD) or through EUS, and some problems are treated with surgery.

Common questions

Will I need my gallbladder removed after ERCP?

Often yes. If stones came from the gallbladder, removing it prevents them coming back.

Are bile duct stents permanent?

Plastic stents are temporary and are usually changed or removed within a few months. Metal stents may stay longer.

Is ERCP the same as MRCP?

No. MRCP is a scan that only takes pictures. ERCP is a procedure that can remove stones and place stents, so it is used when treatment is needed.

How long will I stay in hospital?

Often one night, sometimes only a few hours. Longer stays are needed if there is infection, pancreatitis or another illness.

Can bile duct stones come back?

They can, especially if the gallbladder still has stones. Removing the gallbladder after ERCP greatly lowers this risk.

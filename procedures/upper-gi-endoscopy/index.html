Upper GI endoscopy

How does an upper GI endoscopy work?

An upper GI endoscopy (also called a gastroscopy or OGD) lets the doctor look directly at the lining of the food pipe (oesophagus), stomach and the first part of the small intestine (duodenum). A thin, flexible tube with a light and camera is passed through the mouth while you lie on your side.

It is the most useful test for persistent acidity, upper belly pain, swallowing problems and bleeding, because it shows the problem directly and allows small tissue samples (biopsies) to be taken painlessly. Many problems can also be treated during the same test, such as stopping a bleeding ulcer or widening a narrowing.

When is an upper GI endoscopy needed?

  • Acidity or upper belly pain that does not settle
  • Difficulty swallowing
  • Vomiting blood or black stools
  • Unexplained anaemia or weight loss
  • Testing for H. pylori and celiac disease
  • Checking for varices in liver disease

How do you prepare for an upper GI endoscopy?

  • Nothing to eat for 6 to 8 hours before. Small sips of plain water are usually allowed until 2 hours before, unless you are told otherwise.
  • Tell the doctor if you take blood thinners, diabetes medicines or insulin, as these may need a change for the day.
  • Bring your old reports, prescriptions and any previous endoscopy reports.
  • If you will have sedation, come with an adult who can take you home.
  • Remove dentures and spectacles before the test.

What happens on the day of an upper GI endoscopy?

A local anaesthetic spray numbs the throat, and you may be given light sedation through a vein. You lie on your left side with a small mouth guard to protect your teeth. The scope is passed gently; you can breathe normally throughout. A little air is used to open up the stomach, which can make you feel full or burp. The test usually takes 5 to 10 minutes, and biopsies are painless.

What happens after an upper GI endoscopy?

  • You rest for 30 to 60 minutes if you had sedation. Do not drive, operate machinery or sign important documents for the rest of the day.
  • Eat and drink once the throat numbness wears off, usually after about an hour. Start with something light.
  • A mild sore throat or bloating for a few hours is common.
  • The doctor explains the findings the same day. Biopsy reports usually take 5 to 7 days.

What are the risks of an upper GI endoscopy?

Upper GI endoscopy is a very safe, routine test. A mild sore throat for a day is common. A reaction to the sedative, bleeding after a biopsy, or a tear in the food pipe or stomach are rare, and the risk is slightly higher when treatment such as dilatation is done during the test.

When should you get medical help straight away after an upper GI endoscopy?

  • Severe chest, neck or belly pain after the test
  • Vomiting blood or black, tarry stools
  • Fever, breathlessness or difficulty swallowing

What are the alternatives to an upper GI endoscopy?

A barium swallow or CT scan can show some problems, but they cannot take biopsies or give treatment. For most symptoms of the food pipe and stomach, endoscopy is the most accurate test.

Common questions

Can I eat after an endoscopy?

Yes, once the throat numbness wears off, usually within an hour. Start with something light.

Will I be awake?

You can choose throat spray only, or light sedation so you are drowsy and relaxed. Most people remember very little.

How long does an endoscopy take?

The test itself takes about 5 to 10 minutes. With preparation and recovery, plan for 1 to 2 hours at the hospital.

Do I need to stop my blood thinner?

Do not stop it on your own. Some blood thinners can continue for a simple endoscopy; others are paused if biopsies or treatment are likely. Your doctor will plan this with the doctor who prescribed them.

Can I go home the same day?

Yes. Routine endoscopy is a day procedure and you go home within a couple of hours.

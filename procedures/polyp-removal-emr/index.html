Polyp removal & EMR

By Dr. Kshitij Lochab, Consultant Gastroenterologist · Updated 17 October 2026

Share on WhatsApp

Polyps and some early flat growths are lifted and removed through the scope, often avoiding surgery altogether.

How it works

Polyps are small growths on the inner lining of the bowel. Most are harmless, but some types (adenomas) can slowly turn into cancer over several years. Removing them during a colonoscopy is one of the most effective ways to prevent colon cancer.

Small polyps are removed with a wire loop (snare) or small forceps. Larger and flat growths are removed by endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR): fluid is injected under the growth to lift it away from the deeper wall, and it is then removed with a snare, sometimes in pieces. Tiny clips may be placed to close the area. EMR is also used for some early flat growths in the stomach and food pipe.

What it is used for

  • Polyps in the large bowel
  • Early flat growths in the bowel, stomach or food pipe
  • Removing tissue in one piece for full testing

Before the procedure

  • Preparation is the same as for colonoscopy: low-fibre diet, clear liquids the day before, and the full bowel cleansing drink.
  • Tell the doctor about blood thinners well in advance. They often need to be paused for polyp removal, but only as planned with your doctor.
  • For large polyps, blood tests may be checked beforehand.
  • Come with an adult who can take you home.

On the day

The procedure is done under sedation during a colonoscopy. Removing small polyps adds only a few minutes. EMR of a large polyp can take 30 to 60 minutes. You feel no pain from the removal itself, as the bowel lining has no pain nerves.

After the procedure

  • Most people go home the same day. After a large EMR, a short hospital stay may be advised.
  • Eat light food on the first day.
  • Avoid heavy lifting and strenuous exercise for a few days after a large EMR.
  • Restart blood thinners only when your doctor advises.
  • The tissue report usually takes about a week and decides when your next colonoscopy should be.

Risks

Bleeding can happen during removal, which is usually stopped at once, or up to about two weeks later, which is uncommon. A tear in the bowel wall is rare and is usually closed with clips through the scope. The risks are higher for large polyps and are explained beforehand.

Get medical help straight away if you have

  • More than a few spoonfuls of fresh blood or clots from the back passage
  • Severe belly pain or a swollen, hard belly
  • Fever, dizziness or fainting

Other tests and options

Surgery to remove a part of the bowel is sometimes needed for very large polyps, or if the tissue report shows cancer that has spread deeper. For most polyps and early flat growths, endoscopic removal avoids an operation.

Common questions

Can EMR avoid surgery?

For many early growths, yes. The final plan depends on the tissue report.

Will I need another colonoscopy?

Yes. A follow-up colonoscopy is advised, usually after 1 to 3 years depending on what was removed.

Are all polyps cancer?

No. Most polyps are not cancer. Some types can become cancer over years, which is why they are removed and tested.

Will removing a polyp hurt?

No. It is done under sedation, and the bowel lining does not feel cutting. Mild bloating afterwards is common.

Can polyps come back?

New polyps can form, and large polyps removed in pieces can regrow at the same spot. Follow-up colonoscopy on time catches these early.

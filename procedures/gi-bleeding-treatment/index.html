Bleeding control

By Dr. Kshitij Lochab, Consultant Gastroenterologist · Updated 10 October 2026

Bleeding from an ulcer or a damaged blood vessel is stopped through the scope with tiny clips, injections or heat, usually without surgery.

What it is used for

  • Bleeding stomach or duodenal ulcers
  • Bleeding from varices in liver disease
  • Bleeding from abnormal blood vessels
  • Bleeding after polyp removal

What to expect

Preparation
Done urgently when needed, after fluids or blood to stabilise
Time
About 20 to 45 minutes
Comfort
Done under sedation
After
A short hospital stay for observation

Risks

Most bleeding is controlled through the scope. Sometimes bleeding returns and a repeat procedure, a radiology procedure or surgery is needed.

Conditions it is used for

H. pylori, gastritis & ulcers →Cirrhosis →

Common questions

What should I do if I vomit blood or pass black stools?

Go to the nearest emergency department straight away, or call 112. Do not wait to see if it settles.

Can the bleeding come back?

Sometimes. Treating the cause, such as clearing H. pylori or stopping painkillers, greatly lowers the risk.

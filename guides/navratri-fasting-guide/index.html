Navratri fasting without acidity: a practical guide

By Dr. Kshitij Lochab, Consultant Gastroenterologist · Updated 15 October 2026

During Navratri, many people change how and when they eat for nine days: long gaps, fried vrat snacks, more tea, and a heavy meal at night. This is the most common reason for acidity, bloating, constipation and weakness during the festival. With a few changes, you can keep your fast and keep your stomach comfortable.

In short

Eat something small every 3 to 4 hours, choose roasted over fried, drink plenty of water, and keep dinner light and early. Buy kuttu and singhara atta fresh from a trusted shop. If you have diabetes, are pregnant, have an ulcer or take regular medicines, talk to your doctor before fasting.

Know your type of vrat

Phalahar

Fruit and milk fast

  • Fruit, curd, milk, makhana and nuts through the day
  • The easiest on the stomach for most people
  • Add sabudana or samak rice once a day for energy

Ekasana

One meal a day

  • One full meal, often in the evening
  • Long gaps can cause acidity and weakness
  • Have fruit or chaach during the day if your tradition allows

Nirjala

No food or water

  • Risk of dehydration, dizziness and low sugar
  • Not advised with diabetes, pregnancy, kidney or heart disease, or in older adults
  • If you keep it, limit it to a day and break it gently

Common problems and what helps

Acidity and heartburn

The most common complaint during Navratri, from long gaps, tea on an empty stomach and fried food.

What helps

  • Start the day with water and a few soaked almonds or a fruit, then have your tea
  • Eat something small every 3 to 4 hours
  • Choose roasted makhana, fruit and curd over pakoras and chips
  • Keep dinner light, by about 8 pm, and wait 2 to 3 hours before lying down
  • If you take acidity medicines, keep taking them as prescribed

See a doctor if acidity does not settle, or if there is pain on swallowing, vomiting or black stools. Chest pain with sweating or breathlessness is an emergency, not acidity.

Constipation

Common with less water, little fibre, and more sabudana and potato.

What helps

  • 8 to 10 glasses of water a day, plus chaach or coconut water
  • Fruit with the skin on, such as guava, apple and pear, and soaked raisins or figs
  • Lauki, pumpkin, cucumber and other vrat vegetables at every meal
  • A daily walk, even while fasting

See a doctor if constipation lasts more than a week, or comes with blood in the stool or severe pain.

Weakness, dizziness and headache

Usually from too little fluid, low sugar, or missing your usual tea or coffee.

What helps

  • Drink water, nimbu paani or coconut water through the day
  • Eat a fruit, a few dates or a handful of nuts if you feel weak
  • Do not skip meals completely if you take medicines for sugar or blood pressure
  • Rest, and avoid long hours standing in the heat or at long pujas on an empty stomach

Break the fast and see a doctor for fainting, confusion, or shaking and sweating, or if weakness does not improve after eating and drinking.

Gas and bloating

Sabudana, kuttu and singhara are filling, and a large portion after a long gap causes bloating.

What helps

  • Smaller portions, eaten slowly
  • Pair sabudana with vegetables and curd
  • Avoid fizzy drinks
  • Take a short walk after meals

Bloating with severe or constant pain, vomiting or fever needs a check-up.

Food safety during Navratri

Festival food is bought in large amounts and stored for days. A few precautions prevent most illness.

Kuttu and singhara atta

Every Navratri, people fall ill after eating stale or adulterated kuttu atta. Buy fresh, sealed packs from a trusted shop, check the date, and do not store opened flour for long. Vomiting, giddiness or loose motions soon after eating kuttu needs prompt medical care.

Milk, paneer and sweets

Buy khoya, paneer and sweets from trusted shops and keep them refrigerated. Do not eat sweets that smell or taste stale. Boil milk before use.

Street vrat thalis and juices

Cut fruit and juices from roadside stalls can spread infections such as hepatitis A and E and typhoid. Prefer home-made food and whole fruit that you wash yourself.

Sendha namak

Rock salt is still salt. With high blood pressure, heart or kidney disease, use it in the same small amounts as normal salt.

Break the fast and get help if you have

  • Fainting, confusion or severe dizziness
  • Shaking, sweating and a racing heart, which can mean low sugar
  • Vomiting that does not stop, or passing very little urine
  • Vomiting blood, or black, tar-like stools
  • Chest pain, or severe upper belly pain
  • Vomiting or giddiness soon after eating kuttu atta

Call 112 or go to the nearest emergency department. A fast can always be resumed later; your health comes first.

Who should talk to a doctor before fasting

Fasting is safe for most healthy adults. For some people it needs planning, and a few should not fast strictly.

Diabetes

Sugar can drop dangerously low or rise high during a fast. Check your sugar more often, and ask your doctor before fasting about the timing and dose of your diabetes medicines. Avoid nirjala fasts.

Pregnancy and breastfeeding

Avoid long or nirjala fasts. Eat every few hours and drink plenty of fluids. Our guide to pregnancy and your gut has more advice.

Ulcer, severe acidity or liver disease

Long gaps can bring back ulcer pain. Choose a fruit-and-curd fast and keep taking your medicines. With cirrhosis, ask your doctor before fasting.

Kidney or heart disease, and older adults

Fluids, salt and medicine timings matter. Some tablets must be taken with food, so check with your doctor before changing how you eat.

Children and teenagers

Growing children should not keep strict fasts. A fruit-and-milk fast for part of the day is a safer way to take part.

What to eat during Navratri

Vrat food can be balanced: a filling base, some protein, vegetables and fluids at each meal.

Eat more

  • Roasted makhana, peanuts and a small handful of nuts
  • Curd, chaach, paneer and milk for protein
  • Vrat vegetables: lauki, pumpkin, cucumber, sweet potato and tomato
  • Fruits: guava, apple, papaya, banana and pomegranate
  • Kuttu, singhara, rajgira and samak in place of wheat and rice
  • Water, nimbu paani and coconut water

Cut down

  • Pakoras, puris, sabudana vada and chips
  • Tea or coffee on an empty stomach
  • Sweets, sweetened lassi and fruit juices
  • Very large meals after long gaps
  • Late, heavy dinners

Good to know

  • Sabudana is mostly starch; pair it with peanuts, vegetables and curd
  • Rajgira and kuttu have more protein and fibre than sabudana
  • With diabetes, keep sabudana, potato and sweets small, and check sugar more often
  • Buy kuttu and singhara atta fresh, in sealed packs

A simple vrat day

Morning
Water first, then a few soaked almonds and your tea
Breakfast
A fruit with a bowl of curd, or a glass of milk
Lunch
Sabudana khichdi with peanuts and vegetables, or samak rice with lauki, and curd
Evening
Roasted makhana or peanuts, with chaach or coconut water
Dinner
Kuttu or rajgira roti with a vegetable, by about 8 pm
Bedtime
A glass of warm milk, if it suits you

After Navratri: break the fast gently

After nine days of changed eating, the stomach needs a gentle return. On the first day after the fast, start with light home food such as khichdi, dal-rice or curd rice, and go back to your usual meals over a day or two.

Festive feasts on Dussehra and after, with fried food and sweets, are a common cause of acidity, bloating and loose motions. Keep portions moderate and eat dinner early.

If acidity, pain or bloating continues for more than two weeks after the festival, or if you have any warning sign, get checked.

Common questions

Is it safe to fast if I have acidity?

Yes, for most people, if you avoid long gaps. A fruit-and-curd fast with small meals every 3 to 4 hours is usually easier on the stomach than a one-meal or nirjala fast.

Can people with diabetes fast in Navratri?

Many can, with planning. Talk to your doctor before the fast about your medicines, check sugar more often, avoid nirjala fasts, and break the fast if your sugar falls low.

Should I stop my medicines while fasting?

No. Do not stop regular medicines on your own. Ask your doctor whether the timing needs to change, as some tablets must be taken with food.

Is sabudana healthy for fasting?

It gives quick energy but little protein or fibre. Eat it in small portions with peanuts, vegetables and curd, and alternate it with kuttu, rajgira or samak.

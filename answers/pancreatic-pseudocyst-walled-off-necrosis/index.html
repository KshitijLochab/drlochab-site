What is a pancreatic pseudocyst or walled-off necrosis?

By Dr. Kshitij Lochab, Consultant Gastroenterologist · Updated 10 October 2026

Short answer

After an attack of acute pancreatitis, fluid or dead tissue can collect around the pancreas. After about four weeks, a wall forms around it: a pseudocyst if it is mostly fluid, or walled-off necrosis if it contains dead tissue. Many settle on their own. Those causing pain, infection or blockage can usually be drained through an endoscope, guided by EUS.

Symptoms that need attention

  • Ongoing belly pain or fullness weeks after pancreatitis
  • Vomiting or not being able to eat, from pressure on the stomach
  • Fever, which may mean the collection is infected
  • Jaundice from pressure on the bile duct

Tests

A CT or MRI scan shows the size and contents of the collection. EUS shows how much solid material is inside and the safest place to drain it.

Treatment

Drainage is best done after the wall has matured, usually about four weeks after the attack, unless infection needs earlier action.

  • Collections that cause no symptoms are often just watched
  • EUS-guided drainage: a stent is placed between the stomach and the collection, so it drains into the stomach without any cut on the belly
  • Walled-off necrosis may need the dead tissue to be cleared through the stent (endoscopic necrosectomy), sometimes in more than one session
  • Antibiotics if infected, good nutrition, and treating the cause of pancreatitis, such as gallstones or alcohol

When to seek urgent care

High fever, severe pain, vomiting blood, black stools or fainting after pancreatitis need emergency care straight away. Call 112.

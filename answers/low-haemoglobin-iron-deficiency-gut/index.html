Low haemoglobin or iron deficiency: when is the stomach or bowel the cause?

By Dr. Kshitij Lochab, Consultant Gastroenterologist · Updated 28 November 2026

Share on WhatsApp

Short answer

Iron deficiency is the commonest cause of low haemoglobin in India. In young women it is most often from heavy periods, pregnancy or a diet low in iron. In men of any age, and in women after menopause, iron deficiency is never "just weakness": the gut is the usual cause, from slow bleeding (an ulcer, piles, a polyp or a growth) or from poor absorption (celiac disease, H. pylori, worms). These people should have the cause found, usually with an endoscopy and a colonoscopy, and not just take iron tablets.

How the gut causes iron deficiency

A small daily loss of blood, too little to change the colour of the stool, is enough to empty the body's iron stores over months.

  • Slow, hidden bleeding that you may never see: ulcers and erosions from painkillers or H. pylori, piles, polyps, a growth in the stomach or colon, or small blood vessels that ooze (angiodysplasia)
  • Poor absorption: celiac disease, H. pylori infection, long-term acidity tablets, and surgery on the stomach or small intestine
  • Worms (hookworm), still common where people walk barefoot or water is unsafe
  • Inflammation of the bowel in ulcerative colitis or Crohn's disease

Who should have the gut checked

Taking iron tablets for months without finding the cause can hide a problem, such as a polyp or an early cancer, that is easier to treat when found early.

  • Every man with iron deficiency anaemia, whatever his age
  • Every woman after menopause with iron deficiency
  • Women before menopause who have stomach or bowel symptoms, are over about 50, have a family history of bowel cancer, or whose iron stays low despite treatment
  • Anyone with black stools, blood in the stool, weight loss or a change in bowel habit together with low iron
  • Anyone whose haemoglobin does not rise, or falls again, after a course of iron

Tests your doctor may advise

First, blood tests confirm that the anaemia is from low iron: a complete blood count, serum ferritin and sometimes iron studies. Most people also need a celiac blood test (tTG IgA), an H. pylori test, a urine test for blood, and kidney and liver tests. When the gut needs to be checked, an upper GI endoscopy and a colonoscopy are usually done on the same day, one after the other, under light sedation. Small biopsies from the small intestine are taken to look for celiac disease. If both are normal and the anaemia continues, a capsule endoscopy can look at the small intestine. Treatment

Black stools are normal on iron tablets. Haemoglobin usually starts to rise within 2 to 4 weeks; tablets are continued for about 3 months after it is normal to refill the stores. Do not start or stop iron, or give it to children, without a doctor's advice.

  • Treat the cause: heal the ulcer, clear H. pylori, a gluten-free diet for celiac disease, remove polyps, treat worms or piles
  • Iron tablets, as advised by your doctor. Taking them once a day or on alternate days is often better tolerated and absorbed than several times a day
  • Take iron with water or a vitamin C-rich fruit such as amla, orange or guava, and keep tea, coffee, milk and calcium tablets at least an hour away
  • Iron by drip (intravenous iron) when tablets are not tolerated, do not work, or the iron is needed quickly

Warning signs: see a doctor soon

Children under 12 with low haemoglobin should be seen by their paediatrician. Vomiting blood, black stools with dizziness or fainting, or chest pain with breathlessness is an emergency: call 112 or go to the nearest emergency department.

  • Black, tarry stools or blood in the stool
  • Breathlessness on walking a short distance, chest pain or a racing heart
  • Weight loss without trying, or loss of appetite
  • Difficulty swallowing, or vomiting that keeps coming back
  • A haemoglobin that keeps falling despite iron

Food for low iron

Eat more

  • Iron-rich foods every day: dals, rajma, chana, soya, green leafy vegetables, ragi and bajra; eggs, chicken, fish or meat if you eat them
  • A vitamin C food with the meal: amla, orange, guava, lemon on dal or salad
  • Jaggery, dates and dry fruits as small extras, not as the main source of iron

Cut down

  • Tea or coffee with meals or with the iron tablet: keep them at least an hour apart
  • Milk, curd or calcium tablets taken together with the iron tablet
  • Painkillers for body aches on your own, which can cause hidden stomach bleeding

Good to know

  • Food alone rarely corrects iron deficiency once haemoglobin is low; tablets or a drip are usually needed while the cause is found.
  • Cooking in an iron kadhai adds a little iron, but it does not replace treatment.

Common questions

What haemoglobin is too low?

As a rough guide, haemoglobin below about 13 in men and 12 in women (11 in pregnancy) is called anaemia. Low ferritin in the blood report confirms that iron stores are empty. Your doctor will read the numbers together with your symptoms.

Can acidity tablets cause low iron?

Long-term acidity tablets can reduce iron absorption a little, but they are rarely the only cause. If you take them every day and your iron is low, your doctor will still look for bleeding, H. pylori or celiac disease.

I am a man with low iron but no stomach problem. Do I really need an endoscopy?

Usually yes. Men lose almost no iron, so iron deficiency in a man points to slow bleeding or poor absorption in the gut, even without any symptoms. An endoscopy and colonoscopy find the cause in many people.

My stools turned black on iron tablets. Is that bleeding?

Black stools are expected on iron. Stools that are tarry, sticky and foul-smelling, or black stools with dizziness or weakness, can mean bleeding: see a doctor the same day, or call 112 if you feel faint.

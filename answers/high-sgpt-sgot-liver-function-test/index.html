High SGPT or SGOT in a liver test: what does it mean?

By Dr. Kshitij Lochab, Consultant Gastroenterologist · Medically reviewed by Dr. Kshitij Lochab on 31 October 2026

Share on WhatsApp

Short answer

SGPT (ALT) and SGOT (AST) are liver enzymes. A raised level means liver cells are irritated or injured. In India the commonest cause is fatty liver; alcohol, viral hepatitis and some medicines or herbal products are other common causes. A mild rise is usually not an emergency, but it should be explained, not ignored.

What the numbers mean

SGPT and SGOT leak into the blood when liver cells are irritated. A mild rise, up to about two to three times the upper limit, is common and is usually due to a long-term problem such as fatty liver. Very high levels, in the hundreds or thousands, suggest a sudden injury such as acute viral hepatitis, a medicine reaction or a blocked bile duct, and need prompt attention.

Other parts of the report matter too: bilirubin (jaundice), albumin and INR (how well the liver is working), and alkaline phosphatase and GGT (bile ducts).

Common causes

Hard exercise or a muscle injury can raise SGOT for a few days without any liver problem.

  • Fatty liver, linked to weight, belly fat, diabetes and cholesterol
  • Alcohol
  • Hepatitis B or C, or recent hepatitis A or E
  • Medicines, including some TB medicines, painkillers taken in excess and herbal or body-building supplements
  • Less common: thyroid problems, celiac disease, autoimmune hepatitis and inherited conditions

What usually happens next

Your doctor may repeat the test, and check hepatitis B and C, blood sugar, cholesterol and thyroid, with an ultrasound of the abdomen. If fatty liver is found, a FibroScan helps check for scarring. Further tests are done if the cause is still unclear or the enzymes stay high.

What you can do

  • Stop alcohol until the cause is known
  • Do not start new herbal or 'liver tonic' products
  • Tell your doctor about every medicine and supplement you take
  • If you are overweight, losing 7 to 10% of body weight often brings enzymes back to normal

When to see a doctor quickly

See a doctor the same day if raised enzymes come with yellow eyes, dark urine, vomiting, confusion, drowsiness or bleeding. In an emergency, call 112.

SGPT and SGOT: full form and what the test shows

SGPT and SGOT are two enzymes in a liver function test (LFT). Newer reports may call them ALT and AST.

  • SGPT: serum glutamic pyruvic transaminase, also called ALT (alanine aminotransferase). It is found mainly in the liver.
  • SGOT: serum glutamic oxaloacetic transaminase, also called AST (aspartate aminotransferase). It is found in the liver and also in muscles, the heart and red blood cells, so it can rise even when the liver is healthy.

They show whether liver cells are irritated or injured, not how well the liver works (that is shown by bilirubin, albumin and INR). A normal SGPT does not rule out every liver problem: some people with long-standing scarring have near-normal enzymes.

How much should SGPT be? Normal range and danger levels

Every lab prints its own normal range, and the report should be read against it. Many labs print an upper limit of about 40 U/L, but studies of healthy people show a lower true limit: the American College of Gastroenterology (2017) suggests about 29 to 33 U/L for men and 19 to 25 U/L for women. So a value just inside the lab range can still be slightly high.

There is no single number at which SGPT becomes dangerous. Doctors look at how many times above the upper limit it is, how quickly it rose, and the rest of the report:

  • Mild rise, up to about 2 to 3 times the upper limit: very common, usually from a long-term cause such as fatty liver or alcohol. Not an emergency, but the cause should be found.
  • Moderate rise, several times the upper limit: needs a doctor's review and tests to find the cause soon, not just a repeat test months later.
  • Very high, in the hundreds or thousands: suggests a sudden injury, such as acute viral hepatitis, a reaction to a medicine or herbal product, or a blocked bile duct. This needs prompt medical care.

The SGPT number alone does not decide how serious the illness is. In hepatitis A it can reach the thousands and the person still recovers fully. A rising bilirubin or INR, confusion or drowsiness are more worrying.

The SGOT:SGPT ratio, and why SGOT alone can rise

In fatty liver and viral hepatitis, SGPT is usually higher than SGOT. When SGOT is more than twice SGPT, alcohol-related liver damage is a common reason. The ratio is only a clue: SGOT can also be higher in cirrhosis from any cause and in muscle problems.

SGOT can rise without any liver disease in these situations:

  • Hard exercise, a heavy gym session, a fall or a muscle injury in the past few days
  • Muscle diseases and an underactive thyroid
  • Breakdown of red blood cells, including when a blood sample is tested late

If SGOT is raised and SGPT is normal, the doctor may first check a muscle enzyme (CK) and thyroid tests.

Symptoms of high SGPT

A raised SGPT causes no symptoms by itself; most people find out from a routine check-up. Any symptoms come from the cause:

  • Fatty liver or a mild long-term rise: usually nothing, sometimes tiredness or a dull heaviness under the right ribs
  • Acute hepatitis: poor appetite, nausea, fever and dark urine, followed by yellow eyes
  • Advanced liver disease: swelling of the belly or feet, easy bruising, muscle loss and changes in sleep

Feeling well does not mean the liver is fine, so follow up a raised result even without symptoms. If raised enzymes come with confusion, unusual drowsiness, vomiting blood, black stools or jaundice that is quickly getting deeper, call 112 or go to the nearest emergency department.

Is there a medicine for high SGPT? Treatment and repeating the test

There is no medicine that safely 'lowers SGPT' for everyone. Treatment is aimed at the cause: weight loss and exercise for fatty liver, stopping alcohol, treating hepatitis B or C, or stopping a harmful medicine or supplement. When the cause is treated, the enzymes come down on their own.

Liver tonics, detox drinks and herbal or ayurvedic powders sold for high SGOT and SGPT have no proven benefit, and some injure the liver. Do not stop a prescribed medicine, such as TB treatment, without asking the doctor who prescribed it.

  • Fasting is not usually needed for SGPT and SGOT, but follow the lab's advice if other tests are done on the same sample.
  • Avoid alcohol and very hard workouts for a few days before the test.
  • Take a list of all medicines, supplements and home remedies to the doctor.
  • A mild rise is usually rechecked after a few weeks, ideally in the same lab, along with tests to find the cause.

Food for a healthier liver

Eat more

  • Vegetables, salads and fruit at every meal, and dal, sprouts or chana for protein
  • Whole grains and millets (atta with chokar, bajra, jowar, ragi) in place of maida
  • Plain coffee without much sugar, if you already like it, seems safe and may help the liver

Cut down

  • Alcohol, until the cause is known and ideally for good
  • Sugar, sweets, cold drinks and packaged juices
  • Fried snacks, bakery items and repeatedly heated oil

Good to know

  • A 'liver detox' or tonic does not lower enzymes and some herbal products raise them.
  • In fatty liver, slow weight loss of 7 to 10% is the most effective treatment.

Common questions

Is a slightly high SGPT dangerous?

Usually not an emergency, but it is a signal. A mild rise most often means fatty liver. The cause should be found and the test repeated, because silent liver scarring can build up over years.

How quickly can SGPT come down?

With weight loss, stopping alcohol and treating the cause, enzymes often improve within 3 to 6 months. After a viral hepatitis, they usually settle within weeks.

Can exercise or a gym supplement raise SGOT?

Yes. Hard exercise can raise SGOT for a few days from muscle, and some body-building supplements can injure the liver. Tell your doctor about workouts and supplements before testing.

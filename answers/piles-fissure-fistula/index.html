Piles, fissure or fistula: what is the difference?

By Dr. Kshitij Lochab, Consultant Gastroenterologist · Updated 10 October 2026

Short answer

Piles are swollen veins in the back passage that often bleed painlessly. A fissure is a small tear that causes sharp pain during and after passing stool. A fistula is a small tunnel from inside the back passage to the skin that leaks pus. All three are common, but bleeding from the back passage should be checked to rule out other causes.

How to tell them apart

  • Piles: bright red blood dripping or on the paper, usually without pain; sometimes a lump that comes out and goes back
  • Fissure: sharp, cutting pain while passing stool and for hours afterwards, often with a streak of blood; usually linked to hard stools
  • Fistula: a painful swelling or boil near the anus that bursts, followed by repeated pus or discharge from a small opening on the skin

Home care that helps piles and fissures

  • Soft stools: more fibre (fruits, vegetables, whole grains, isabgol) and enough water
  • Do not strain or sit on the toilet for long, including with a phone
  • Warm water sitz baths for 10 to 15 minutes, two or three times a day
  • Go when you feel the urge, without delay

Treatment

  • Piles: creams for short-term relief; rubber band ligation, a quick clinic procedure, for bleeding piles that do not settle; surgery for large piles
  • Fissure: stool softening and medicated ointments heal most fissures in a few weeks; long-standing fissures may need further treatment
  • Fistula: usually needs surgery, as it rarely heals on its own

When to see a doctor

See a doctor for any bleeding from the back passage that is new, keeps coming back, or comes with a change in bowel habit, weight loss, anaemia or a family history of bowel cancer, especially after age 45. Do not assume it is piles; a colonoscopy may be needed. Heavy bleeding, fever with a painful swelling, or dizziness needs urgent care.

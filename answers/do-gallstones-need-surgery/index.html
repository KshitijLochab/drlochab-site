Do gallstones always need an operation?

By Dr. Kshitij Lochab, Consultant Gastroenterologist · Updated 17 October 2026

Short answer

No. Gallstones found by chance that have never caused symptoms can usually be left alone. If stones cause pain attacks or complications, removing the gallbladder by keyhole surgery is the usual treatment, because attacks tend to come back. Stones that slip into the bile duct are usually removed by ERCP, without a cut.

When stones can be left alone

Many people learn they have gallstones from an ultrasound done for another reason. If the stones have never caused pain, the chance of trouble is low, and doctors usually advise watching rather than an operation.

In a few situations your doctor may still suggest surgery without symptoms, such as very large stones, a gallbladder polyp, or changes in the gallbladder wall on the scan. This is decided after looking at your reports.

When an operation is usually advised

An operation is usually advised if stones cause:

  • Repeated attacks of pain: strong pain in the upper right or middle of the belly, often after a heavy meal, lasting from half an hour to a few hours
  • A swollen or infected gallbladder (cholecystitis)
  • A stone slipping into the bile duct, causing jaundice or infection. This stone is first removed by ERCP, a scope passed through the mouth
  • Pancreatitis caused by a stone

Can medicines or home remedies dissolve them?

Rarely. A medicine can slowly dissolve a few small cholesterol stones over many months, but it does not work for most people, and the stones often return once it is stopped.

Lemon water, apple juice, oil "flushes" and other home remedies do not remove gallstones, and some can bring on a painful attack. Diet cannot dissolve stones, though lighter, regular meals may mean fewer attacks.

About the operation, and when it is urgent

Once stones have caused pain, attacks usually come back and the risk of complications rises. The usual operation is keyhole (laparoscopic) removal of the gallbladder, and most people go home within a day or two.

The gallbladder only stores bile; the liver keeps making it. Most people eat normally afterwards. A few have loose motions for some weeks, which usually settles.

Go to the nearest emergency department or call 112 if belly pain lasts more than a few hours, or comes with fever and shivering, yellow eyes or skin, or repeated vomiting.

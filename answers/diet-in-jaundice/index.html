What should I eat in jaundice?

By Dr. Kshitij Lochab, Consultant Gastroenterologist · Medically reviewed by Dr. Kshitij Lochab on 31 October 2026

Share on WhatsApp

Short answer

Eat normal, balanced home food with enough calories, and drink plenty of clean water. There is no need to avoid haldi, oil or salt used in normal cooking. Avoid alcohol completely, and do not take herbal remedies unless your doctor approves them.

Good choices

  • Dal, rice, roti, khichdi and vegetables
  • Curd, buttermilk and fruits
  • Small meals every few hours if appetite is poor
  • Plenty of boiled or filtered water

Myths to ignore

  • Boiled food only: this causes weakness and slows recovery
  • No haldi or oil: normal cooking amounts are fine
  • Sugarcane juice or special home remedies cure jaundice: they do not, and unclean juice can spread infection

What to avoid

  • Alcohol, completely, until your doctor says the liver has recovered, and longer for some causes
  • Herbal products and medicines not prescribed by your doctor
  • Street food and water from unsafe sources

Find the cause

Jaundice is a sign, not a disease. Most cases from hepatitis A or E settle in weeks, but a blocked bile duct, hepatitis B or a medicine reaction need specific treatment. Get liver tests and an ultrasound early.

1What to eat in jaundice

In jaundice from viral hepatitis, the liver heals well when the body gets enough energy and protein. Normal home food, in small portions if appetite is poor, is the right diet. Some easy choices through the day:

  • Breakfast: poha, upma, idli, dalia, oats, or toast with an egg
  • Lunch and dinner: roti or rice with dal and a simple sabzi, khichdi, or curd rice
  • In between: fruits washed and cut at home (papaya, banana, apple, orange, pomegranate), curd or chaas, roasted chana
  • Fluids: boiled or filtered water, coconut water, lemon water and clear soups; ORS if there is vomiting or loose motions

Poha is a good choice in jaundice: light, easy to digest and filling. Cook it at home with normal amounts of oil and haldi, with peanuts or vegetables. Yellow foods such as haldi, dal, papaya or mango do not make jaundice worse; the yellow colour of the eyes comes from bilirubin in the blood, not from the colour of food.

2The real parhej in jaundice: what not to eat or take

Many families stop oil, haldi, salt, non-veg or yellow foods during jaundice. None of these needs stopping in normal amounts. The real parhej is:

  • Alcohol, completely, for at least 6 months after recovery, and for good in some liver conditions
  • Herbal, ayurvedic or desi jaundice medicines, liver tonics, and unknown powders or pills from local healers. Some of these injure the liver and can turn a mild illness into a severe one.
  • Street food, cut fruit, pani puri, ice and water from unsafe sources, which spread hepatitis A and E
  • Painkillers and other medicines not advised by your doctor, including ones bought without a prescription. Do not stop regular medicines for diabetes, blood pressure or thyroid on your own; ask your doctor.

Very oily, fried or rich restaurant food can make nausea worse and is better kept aside until appetite returns.

3Should you walk or rest in jaundice?

Strict bed rest is not needed for most people with jaundice from hepatitis A or E. Rest when tired, sleep well, and do light activity as the body allows, such as moving around the house or a short, slow walk. Gentle activity helps appetite and stops muscles from getting weak.

Avoid heavy exercise, the gym and sports until energy returns and the doctor is satisfied with the liver tests. Anyone very weak, vomiting, or with deepening jaundice needs more rest and a doctor's review.

Hepatitis A and E spread through stool. Until the doctor says it is safe, the person should wash hands with soap after the toilet and not cook for others. Children usually return to school once they feel well and the doctor agrees.

4Treatment of jaundice, and how long recovery takes

Jaundice is treated by treating its cause. Hepatitis A and E have no specific medicine; the liver heals on its own with food, fluids and rest, while the doctor follows the liver tests. A stone in the bile duct is removed by endoscopy (ERCP), hepatitis B may need long-term medicines, and a medicine reaction needs that medicine stopped.

  • Nausea and poor appetite often start to ease soon after the jaundice appears, usually within one to two weeks.
  • The yellow colour of the eyes fades more slowly, often over a few weeks.
  • Most people with hepatitis A or E recover fully within about one to three months, although tiredness can last a little longer.
  • In a few people, hepatitis A comes back briefly after seeming to settle, or the jaundice lasts longer with itching. These people also usually recover fully, with a doctor's follow-up.

A home remedy taken at the same time often gets the credit for a recovery that was going to happen anyway.

5When jaundice needs urgent care

See a doctor the same day for any jaundice in pregnancy, jaundice in someone who already has liver disease, jaundice with high fever and shivering, or jaundice that is still getting worse after two weeks.

  • Confusion, unusual sleepiness or a change in behaviour
  • Bleeding from the gums or nose, vomiting blood, or black stools
  • Repeated vomiting, being unable to keep food or water down, or passing very little urine
  • Severe belly pain

These can be signs of severe liver injury or a serious infection. Call 112 or go to the nearest emergency department.

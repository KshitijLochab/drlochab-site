What is abdominal (intestinal) tuberculosis?

By Dr. Kshitij Lochab, Consultant Gastroenterologist · Updated 10 October 2026

Short answer

Tuberculosis (TB) can affect the intestines, the lining of the abdomen (causing fluid), and lymph nodes in the belly. It is common in India. It causes weight loss, fever, night sweats, belly pain and changes in bowel habit. It can be cured with a full course of TB medicines, which are free under the national TB programme, but the diagnosis should be confirmed first.

Symptoms

  • Weight loss, loss of appetite and tiredness
  • Low-grade fever and night sweats
  • Belly pain, often around the lower right side
  • Loose motions or constipation, or episodes of blockage with vomiting and swelling
  • A swollen belly from fluid (peritoneal TB)

How it is diagnosed

  • Colonoscopy with biopsies from the lower small intestine and colon, tested for TB germs (including GeneXpert), culture and the typical microscopic changes
  • CT scan of the abdomen
  • Testing the belly fluid (including ADA) when there is ascites
  • Chest X-ray, as TB may also be in the lungs

TB or Crohn's disease?

Intestinal TB and Crohn's disease can look very similar on colonoscopy and scans. The treatments are very different, and the wrong one can cause harm: steroids and immune medicines for Crohn's can make TB worse. So doctors try hard to confirm the diagnosis before starting treatment, and sometimes follow up closely after a trial of TB medicines.

Treatment

Never stop TB medicines early because you feel better. Incomplete treatment can lead to drug-resistant TB.

  • A full course of TB medicines, usually for at least six months, taken every day without gaps
  • Treatment is free under the National TB Elimination Programme (Ni-kshay), and patients also receive nutrition support
  • Regular follow-up to check the response and watch for side effects, especially on the liver
  • Surgery only for complications such as severe narrowing or perforation

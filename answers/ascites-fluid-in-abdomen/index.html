What is ascites (fluid in the abdomen), and how is it treated?

By Dr. Kshitij Lochab, Consultant Gastroenterologist · Updated 10 October 2026

Short answer

Ascites is a build-up of fluid inside the abdomen. The commonest cause is cirrhosis of the liver; tuberculosis, cancer, heart failure and kidney disease are other causes. It is treated by finding the cause, cutting down salt, tablets that remove extra water, and draining the fluid when needed.

Symptoms

  • A belly that swells over weeks, with weight gain
  • Tight clothes, a feeling of fullness and poor appetite
  • Breathlessness when the fluid is large
  • Swelling of the feet

Tests

An ultrasound confirms the fluid. A small sample is usually taken with a thin needle (diagnostic tap) to find the cause and check for infection. Blood tests, liver tests and sometimes an endoscopy for varices are done when cirrhosis is the cause.

Treatment

Drinking water is not usually restricted unless blood sodium is low. Avoid painkillers such as NSAIDs, which can harm the kidneys in people with ascites.

  • Cut down salt: no added salt at the table, and avoid pickles, papad, namkeen, packaged and restaurant food. Most people are advised to keep salt to about one teaspoon (5 g) a day in total.
  • Tablets that help the kidneys remove extra salt and water (diuretics), with regular blood tests
  • Removing large amounts of fluid with a needle (therapeutic paracentesis), often with albumin given through a drip
  • Treating the cause, and stopping alcohol completely
  • In repeated or difficult cases, a TIPS procedure or assessment for liver transplant

Warning signs

Fever, belly pain or tenderness, confusion or passing very little urine can mean infection of the fluid or kidney problems. These need urgent care: go to the nearest emergency department or call 112.

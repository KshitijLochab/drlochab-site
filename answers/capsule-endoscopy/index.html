What is capsule endoscopy (the pill camera)?

By Dr. Kshitij Lochab, Consultant Gastroenterologist · Updated 10 October 2026

Short answer

Capsule endoscopy uses a camera the size of a large tablet, which you swallow. It takes thousands of pictures as it passes through the small intestine, a part that a normal endoscopy or colonoscopy cannot fully reach. It is mainly used to find hidden bleeding, unexplained anaemia and Crohn's disease of the small bowel.

Why it is done

  • Bleeding or iron-deficiency anaemia when endoscopy and colonoscopy are normal
  • Suspected Crohn's disease of the small intestine
  • Small bowel growths or polyps
  • Sometimes, celiac disease that is not responding to diet

Preparation and the day

You usually take a clear liquid diet the day before, sometimes with a bowel preparation, and fast overnight. Sensors or a belt recorder are attached, and you swallow the capsule with water. You can go about light activities; clear fluids are allowed after about 2 hours and a light meal after about 4 hours. The recorder is returned after about 8 to 12 hours, and the capsule passes in the stool and is flushed away.

Risks and limits

  • The main risk is the capsule getting stuck at a narrowing, which is uncommon. In people with suspected narrowing, a dissolvable 'patency' capsule or a scan is used first.
  • It cannot take biopsies or treat problems. If something is found, a special scope (enteroscopy) or other treatment may be needed.
  • Tell the doctor if you have a pacemaker, swallowing problems, or previous bowel surgery or blockage.

Results

The doctor reviews the video, which takes time, and the results are usually explained within a few days.

What is bile duct cancer (cholangiocarcinoma)?

By Dr. Kshitij Lochab, Consultant Gastroenterologist · Updated 10 October 2026

Short answer

Cholangiocarcinoma is a cancer of the bile ducts, inside or outside the liver. It often shows up as painless, deepening jaundice with itching, pale stools and weight loss. Tests such as MRCP, CT and EUS find it, and a sample taken during EUS or ERCP confirms it. Surgery can cure early cases; stents relieve jaundice in others.

Symptoms

  • Yellow eyes and skin that keep getting deeper, often without pain
  • Itching, dark urine and pale stools
  • Weight loss, loss of appetite and tiredness
  • Sometimes fever if the blocked bile becomes infected

Who is at higher risk

  • Long-standing inflammation of the bile ducts, such as primary sclerosing cholangitis
  • Choledochal cysts
  • Bile duct stones present for many years
  • Cirrhosis and chronic hepatitis B or C

Tests

Blood tests including liver tests and the tumour marker CA 19-9, an ultrasound, and then MRCP and CT to see where the duct is blocked and whether the cancer has spread. EUS and ERCP allow brushings or biopsies to confirm the diagnosis.

Treatment

  • Surgery offers the best chance of cure when the cancer is found early and has not spread
  • Stents placed by ERCP or EUS, or drainage through the skin, relieve jaundice and itching
  • Chemotherapy and other medicines help control the disease
  • Care is planned by a team, including a gastroenterologist, surgeon and oncologist

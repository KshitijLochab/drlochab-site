Can TB medicines damage the liver?

By Dr. Kshitij Lochab, Consultant Gastroenterologist · Updated 10 October 2026

Share on WhatsApp

Short answer

Some TB medicines can irritate or damage the liver in a small number of people, usually in the first two months of treatment. Warning signs are loss of appetite, nausea, vomiting, belly pain, dark urine and yellow eyes. If these appear, contact your doctor straight away for liver tests. TB treatment is then adjusted and safely restarted under supervision, because TB itself must still be treated.

Who is at higher risk

  • People who drink alcohol
  • People with hepatitis B or C, fatty liver or other liver disease
  • Older people, and people who are underweight or poorly nourished
  • People taking other medicines that affect the liver, including herbal products

Warning signs

Rifampicin, a TB medicine, normally turns urine and tears orange-red. That alone is harmless and is not a sign of liver damage.

  • Loss of appetite, nausea or vomiting
  • Pain in the upper right belly
  • Dark urine, yellow eyes or skin
  • Unusual tiredness

What to do

Contact your TB doctor the same day if warning signs appear; do not wait for the next visit. Liver blood tests are done, and the medicines may be stopped for a while. Once the liver recovers, the medicines are usually restarted one at a time, or replaced, under close watch. Do not stop or restart TB medicines on your own.

Lowering the risk

  • Avoid alcohol completely during TB treatment
  • Get liver tests and hepatitis B and C checked before starting if you are at higher risk
  • Tell your doctor about every other medicine and supplement
  • Keep follow-up visits and blood tests as advised

Protecting the liver during TB treatment

Eat more

  • Regular, balanced meals with enough protein: dal, milk, curd, paneer, eggs
  • Fruits and vegetables every day
  • Plenty of water

Cut down

  • Alcohol, completely, for the whole course
  • Herbal, Ayurvedic or 'liver protection' products unless your doctor approves
  • Painkillers and other medicines without telling your TB doctor

Good to know

  • Take TB medicines exactly as advised; if they upset your stomach, ask before changing the timing.
  • Being underweight raises the risk of liver injury, so good nutrition protects the liver too.

Common questions

Should I take a liver tonic to protect my liver?

No. There is no proven benefit, and some herbal products can themselves injure the liver. Regular check-ups and avoiding alcohol protect the liver better.

Is the liver damage permanent?

Usually not. When it is picked up early and the medicines are stopped in time, the liver recovers fully in most people.

Can I continue TB treatment if I have fatty liver or hepatitis B?

Yes, but with closer liver tests. Hepatitis B may need its own treatment alongside. Your doctor will plan the safest combination.

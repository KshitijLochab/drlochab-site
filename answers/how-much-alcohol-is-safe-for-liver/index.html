How much alcohol is safe for the liver?

By Dr. Kshitij Lochab, Consultant Gastroenterologist · Updated 10 October 2026

Share on WhatsApp

Short answer

No amount of alcohol is completely safe, and the risk to the liver rises with every drink. Drinking every day, binge drinking at weekends, being a woman, being overweight, or having fatty liver or hepatitis B or C all raise the risk. People with any liver disease should not drink at all. Liver damage is silent until late, so check-ups matter.

What counts as one drink

Each has roughly 10 to 14 grams of alcohol. Pours at home and at parties are often double this.

  • One 30 ml peg of whisky, rum or vodka
  • One small 330 ml bottle of regular beer; a 650 ml bottle is about two, and strong beer more
  • One glass (about 150 ml) of wine

Who should not drink at all

  • Anyone with fatty liver, hepatitis B or C, cirrhosis or a past episode of alcoholic hepatitis or pancreatitis
  • Pregnant women
  • People taking medicines that interact with alcohol, including some TB medicines
  • Teenagers

Common myths

  • 'Beer is light, so it is safe': the alcohol is the same, only diluted
  • 'I only drink at weekends': four or five drinks in one sitting harms the liver more than spreading them out
  • 'Good-quality whisky does not harm the liver': the liver reacts to alcohol, not the brand
  • 'Liver tonics protect the liver': they do not, and some harm it

Checking your liver, and cutting down

If you drink regularly, ask for liver tests, an ultrasound and a FibroScan. The liver can recover a lot when drinking stops early. If you find it hard to cut down, ask your doctor about de-addiction support. People who drink heavily every day should not stop suddenly without medical advice, as withdrawal can be dangerous.

Food, drink and the liver

Eat more

  • Alcohol-free days every week, if you drink at all
  • Water or nimbu paani between drinks, and food before drinking
  • Vegetables, dal and whole grains to keep weight and sugar in control

Cut down

  • Binge drinking: four or more drinks in one sitting
  • Fried snacks, namkeen and sweet mixers with drinks, which add to fatty liver
  • Drinking with painkillers or sleeping pills

Good to know

  • Eating while drinking slows absorption but does not protect the liver.
  • Coffee does not cancel out alcohol.

Common questions

Is red wine good for the heart and liver?

Any heart benefit is small and uncertain, and alcohol harms the liver. Do not start drinking for health reasons.

How long after stopping alcohol does the liver recover?

Fatty liver from alcohol can improve within weeks of stopping. Early scarring can improve over months. Cirrhosis does not fully reverse, but stopping still improves survival.

My liver tests are normal. Does that mean my liver is fine?

Not always. Liver tests can be normal even with scarring. A FibroScan gives a better picture if you drink regularly.

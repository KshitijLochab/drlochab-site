Do not wait

Get medical care today if you have

In an emergency, call 112 or go to the nearest hospital emergency department.

  • Vomiting of blood or coffee-ground material
  • Black, sticky, tar-like stools
  • Yellow eyes with fever, confusion or drowsiness
  • Sudden, severe belly pain with vomiting
  • Food stuck in the chest, unable to swallow saliva
  • Heavy bleeding from the back passage

Acidity

Raise the head of your bed

For night-time reflux, raising the head end of the bed by 6 to 8 inches helps gravity keep acid down. Extra pillows only bend the neck and do not work as well.

Try this todayPlace bricks or wooden blocks under the legs at the head end of your bed.

Habits

Eat slowly, swallow less air

Eating fast, talking while eating, and drinking through straws make you swallow air, which adds to bloating.

Try this todayPut your spoon down between bites today and take at least 15 minutes over lunch.

Myth Buster

Myth or fact?

“Every gallstone needs an operation.”

Myth: Silent gallstones often need no surgery. Many gallstones are found by chance on an ultrasound and never cause trouble. Surgery is usually advised once stones cause pain or complications such as infection, jaundice or pancreatitis.

Tests & procedures

What to expect from an endoscopy

Most procedures use a thin, flexible tube with a camera. There are no cuts, and most people go home the same day. Tap any one to see how it works.

Upper GI endoscopyA thin camera passed through the mouth to look at the food pipe, stomach and first part of the intestine. Small painless biopsies can be taken.ColonoscopyA camera passed through the back passage to examine the large bowel. Polyps found during the test are removed at the same time.Endoscopic ultrasoundAn ultrasound probe on the tip of the scope gives close images of the pancreas, bile duct and nearby glands from inside the body. It can take needle samples and drain fluid collections.ERCPA scope with X-ray guidance reaches the opening of the bile and pancreatic ducts to remove stones and place stents through blockages, without any cuts.Variceal bandingIn liver disease, veins in the food pipe can swell and bleed. Small rubber bands placed through the scope shrink them and prevent or stop bleeding.Polyp removal & EMRPolyps and some early flat growths are lifted and removed through the scope, often avoiding surgery altogether.Bleeding controlBleeding from an ulcer or a damaged blood vessel is stopped through the scope with tiny clips, injections or heat, usually without surgery.Feeding tube (PEG)For people who cannot swallow safely, a soft feeding tube is placed through the skin into the stomach with the help of the scope.Dilatation & stentingNarrowed sections of the food pipe, bile duct or bowel are widened with a balloon, or kept open with a stent placed through the scope.Capsule endoscopyYou swallow a camera the size of a large tablet. It takes thousands of pictures of the small intestine, a part a normal endoscope cannot fully reach, and passes out naturally.

Before you visit

Common questions

Do I need a referral to see a gastroenterologist?

No. You can book directly. If another doctor has seen you, bring their notes and prescriptions.

What should I bring to my first consultation?

All previous reports, scans, endoscopy and biopsy reports, and a list of every medicine and supplement you take. Photos of reports on your phone are fine.

Do I need to come fasting?

Not for a consultation. Fasting is needed only for procedures such as endoscopy, and for some blood tests. You will be told in advance.

Are video consultations available?

Yes. They work well for follow-ups, reviewing reports and diet advice. A first visit for new symptoms is better in person, so that you can be examined.

Is endoscopy safe?

Endoscopy is a routine and very safe procedure. Serious complications are uncommon. The benefits and risks for your situation are explained before any procedure, and you can ask any question you have.

Where are endoscopy and other procedures done?

Procedures are done in a fully equipped hospital endoscopy unit with monitoring and anaesthesia support. The details are shared at your consultation.
